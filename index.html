How I work

The same five steps, whatever the industry. Most failed ML projects skip the first and the last.

  1. Frame the decision

    Agree what a miss costs against a false alarm, and what a win is worth, before writing code.

  2. Find the signal

    Turn messy logs, telemetry and transactions into features that move ahead of the event.

  3. Build for rare events

    Models tuned for imbalanced data, judged on catch rate and precision, not accuracy.

  4. Run it in production

    Pipelines, versioning, CI/CD and drift monitoring that triggers retraining.

  5. Prove the money

    Control groups and business metrics, so the value is measured, not assumed.

Selected work

Three projects in depth: the decision each one supported, the hard part, and what it delivered.

Catching device component failures 7–14 days ahead

Fortune 500 PC manufacturer · at Sutherland

~$500K a yearestimated savings from 15% fewer warranty claims

Same pattern in: energy assets, telecom networks, fleets, data centres

The decision
Which devices should the service team fix before they break at the customer's desk?
The hard part
Failures are rare, so a model can look accurate while missing almost all of them. I agreed the real trade-off with the client up front: the cost of a missed failure against the cost of a false alarm.
What I built
Drift-adaptive gradient-boosted models (XGBoost, LightGBM) on features from 500GB+ of device logs a day, processed with PySpark on Databricks. I owned the solution architecture and led the team that delivered it.
Result
83% precision, 7–14 days ahead. Scores 250K+ devices (1M+ component predictions) daily in production, with MLflow, CI/CD and drift monitoring. Warranty claims fell 15% and customer satisfaction rose 10%.

Turning churn risk into retained premium

Insurance · at Duck Creek Technologies

$600Kannual premium retained, churn down 18%

Same pattern in: banking attrition, credit risk, subscriptions, telecom

The decision
Which policyholders should retention teams contact, and what should they say?
The hard part
A risk score alone doesn't change behaviour. Underwriters needed to see why a customer was at risk, and leadership needed proof the campaigns worked rather than a correlation.
What I built
Risk and churn models that improved AUC by 12% over production, with SHAP reason codes for each customer, and a ranked list of at-risk policyholders for the retention teams.
Result
A/B tested against a control group: churn fell 18% in six months, renewals rose 22%, and $600K in annual premium was retained.

An AI copilot for 1,500 employees

Global health organisation · at PATH

~600 hourssaved every month, support dependency down 40%

Same pattern in: bank operations, policy and compliance teams, internal helpdesks

The decision
How do 1,500 people get reliable answers from policy, finance and HR documents without waiting on a support team?
The hard part
An assistant that answers confidently but wrongly is worse than none. Answers had to come from the organisation's own documents.
What I built
A retrieval-augmented (RAG) copilot on Azure OpenAI and LangChain that answers from the organisation's own documents.
Result
Support-team dependency fell 40% across 1,500+ employees, about 600 hours saved a month. I also led the organisation's move to Microsoft Fabric as its single data and AI platform.
